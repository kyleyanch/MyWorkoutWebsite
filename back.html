Width, thickness, and posture
Primary mover: Latissimus Dorsi

A strong back is the engine of posture, pulling power, and heavy deadlifts. The latissimus dorsi does the heavy lifting here — every exercise in this library keys off the lats, with the biceps assisting as secondary movers. Train both directions: vertical pulls (pull-ups and pulldowns) build width, while rows and deadlift patterns build thickness and overall strength.

Training tips

Lead with the shoulder blades

Retract your shoulder blades before you pull so the back, not the biceps, does the work. Scapular control is the whole game.
Width AND thickness

Vertical pulls give you wide lats; horizontal rows build a dense mid-back. Do not pick one at the expense of the other.
Protect your spine

Brace your core on every row and pull. Never let your lower back round under load — that is how hernias and tweaks happen.
Respect grip fatigue

Straps are a tool, not a cheat. Use them on heavy rows and deadlifts so grip never limits your back training.

Common questions

Cannot do a pull-up yet?

Use lat pulldowns, inverted rows, and band-assisted variations, and add slow negative pull-ups (3–5 second lowers) to build toward your first strict rep.

Pendlay row or barbell row?

The Pendlay row is stricter and starts from the floor every rep; a standard barbell row uses a bit more hinge and momentum. Use both across the week.

How often should I train back?

One to three times per week depending on how many of your other lifts involve pulling. Spread the volume out and keep quality high.
