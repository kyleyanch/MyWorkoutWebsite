Three heads, one healthy joint
Primary mover: Deltoids

The deltoids power every press and every raise — and they take a beating from bench and overhead work alike. Train all three heads: presses for the front and overall mass, lateral raises for width, and rear-delt work for balance and posture. Because the shoulder is a ball-and-socket joint with limited room, warm it up well and keep overhead loads sensible.

Training tips

Warm up first

Band pull-aparts and light rear-delt work before pressing keep the joint happy and the rotator cuff ready.
Do not lock out overhead

Keep a slight bend in your elbows and press the weight in line with your ears. Locking out shifts stress into the joint.
Do not neglect rear delts

Face pulls and reverse flies balance out the front-heavy pressing work and improve posture at the same time.
Raise with control

Lateral and front raises stop at shoulder height with no swinging. Form beats ego on raises — the weight should feel light.

straight answers

Common questions

Pressing hurts my shoulder

Reduce the load, keep your elbows slightly in front of your torso, and strengthen the rear delts and rotator cuff. Persistent pain means stop and see a professional.

How often should I train shoulders?

Press one to two times per week. Because raises are light and recover fast, you can slot extra lateral and rear-delt work onto other days.

What weight for lateral raises?

Light. Strict, controlled reps with moderate weight build the shoulders far better than heavy swinging sets.
