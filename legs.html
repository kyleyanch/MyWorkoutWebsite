Your legs are the engine of everything you do in the gym. This library covers full squat patterns, hinges, lunges, and calf work to build quadriceps strength, strong glutes, and a resilient posterior chain. Training tips

Descend with control

Squat deep enough to feel real tension, keep your heels down, and let your knees track over your toes. Depth beats ego.
Do not skip the hinge

Romanian and conventional deadlifts build the hamstrings and glutes that squatting alone misses. Hinge work keeps the posterior chain strong.
Progress weight gradually

Leg day is the most taxing session of the week. Add load in small steps and protect your form on every set.
Go one-legged sometimes

Split squats, lunges, and single-leg presses expose and fix left-right imbalances before they turn into injuries.

straight answers

Common questions

How deep should I squat?

As deep as you can while keeping a neutral spine and your heels down. Parallel is a solid goal; deeper is fine if your hips allow it.

Squats or deadlifts first?

Alternate weekly. Whichever lift you want to improve goes first while you are fresh and strongest.

My knees hurt when I squat

Lower the load, warm up thoroughly, and keep your knees tracking over your toes. Sharp or persistent pain is a cue to check form or see a professional.

How often should I train legs?

One to two quality sessions per week. Legs recover more slowly than smaller muscle groups, so respect the rest.
