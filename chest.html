Build a powerful chest
Primary mover: Pectoralis Major

Your chest is built on the pectoralis major — the driving muscle behind every push. Balanced chest training pairs heavy compound presses (barbell, dumbbell, machine) with fly variations that isolate the pecs through a full stretch. Start with a weight you can control for every rep, and add load only when your form stays locked in.

Training tips

Press before you fly

Do compound presses first while you are fresh, then finish with flies. That way you load the presses harder and still get the isolation in at the end.
Watch your elbow angle

A roughly 30–45 degree elbow tuck spares your shoulders. Flare your elbows too wide and the front delts take over the movement.
Own the eccentric

Lower the bar or dumbbells with control over 2–3 seconds. Bouncing off the chest throws away half the growth stimulus.
Change the angle

Incline work hits the upper chest, decline hits the lower. Rotate angles across sessions for balanced development.

Common questions

Barbell or dumbbell press?

Both. The barbell lets you move more weight, while dumbbells allow a deeper stretch and correct side-to-side imbalances. Rotate them across the week.

How often should I train chest?

One to two times per week with at least 48 hours between sessions is plenty for growth. More volume with poor recovery does not help.

Why do my shoulders hurt when pressing?

Usually too much load, flared elbows, or a weak back. Drop the weight, tuck your elbows, and brace your shoulder blades into the bench before every rep. Persistent pain means see a professional.

Do I need a spotter?

For heavy barbell pressing, yes. A spotter or safety rack lets you push close to failure without risk of a failed rep.
